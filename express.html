A student-powered infrastructure for opportunity in Washington, DC.

Student-powered. Institutionally grounded. Built to last.

BRIDGE the District develops college students into community leaders, then uses their closeness to younger students to open doors to the people, institutions and experiences that make opportunity real. Opportunity should not depend on who you already know.

Executive summary

  • What: a student-powered 501(c)(3) in Washington, DC, founded in 2025, closing opportunity gaps for youth within and beyond the classroom.
  • How: college students train as Impact Scholars, then design and lead career, civic and community programs, and bring younger students along.
  • Demand: 101 students applied for 40 spring 2027 Impact Scholar seats (Applications, Oct 26 to Nov 23, 2026).
  • Standing: incorporated in DC (July 2025) and recognized as a 501(c)(3) public charity, EIN 39-3160840.
  • Spring 2027: 10 planned programs, January to April, with a $10,000 season goal.
  • The ask: partnerships from $250 to $10,000+, unrestricted or program-specific, plus mentors, volunteers and in-kind support.

Why it exists

DC is full of open doors. Not every student gets to walk through them.

  • 55%of DC's public high school class of 2024 enrolled in college or another postsecondary program within six months of graduating, down a point from the year before. Source
  • 80%+of jobs in the District of Columbia are projected to require education or training beyond high school by 2031, the highest share in the country. Source
  • 22% vs 57%Who took AP courses in DC, 2021–22: Black students 22%, White students 57%. Source

Who it serves

College students
Impact Scholars from Howard, American, George Washington and other DC-area universities, across three tracks, who gain leadership experience, professional exposure, mentors and networks.
Younger students
Students in DC schools who gain enrichment, college exposure, mentorship and resources, led by near-peers.
The community
Professionals, schools and institutions who meet students as colleagues, not an audience.

What makes the model different

For students. By students. With students.

  1. ResourcesInstitutions bring what students cannot easily reach alone: rooms, expertise, funding and introductions.
  2. ProgrammingCareer programs, community nights, school visits and Impact Scholar training, run on one season calendar across three tracks.
  3. Impact ScholarsThe transfer station. College students train as Impact Scholars, then design, host and lead each program. Every investment passes through them.
  4. College studentsCollege students gain leadership experience, professional exposure, mentors and access to professional networks.
  5. Younger studentsYounger students gain college exposure, near-peer mentors, enrichment and access to resources.
  6. OutcomesStronger educational pathways, wider networks, more access to institutions and a more connected city.

Due diligence at a glance

Student-powered. Institutionally grounded.

Standing and governance

  • Incorporated in the District of Columbia as a nonprofit corporation on July 9, 2025.
  • Recognized as a 501(c)(3) public charity, EIN 39-3160840. Recognized by the IRS in September 2025, effective July 9, 2025. Gifts are tax-deductible as allowed by law.
  • BRIDGE is governed by bylaws adopted at incorporation, which provide for a volunteer Board of Directors of three to fifteen members. Board seats have not yet been filled. Day to day, the Executive Director leads a student leadership team.

Built for continuity

  • Returning Track Leads Returning Impact Scholars help lead the three tracks, so experience carries from one cohort to the next.
  • A written library The BRIDGE Library holds step-by-step guides, such as planning a program and hosting a school visit on campus, so no program depends on one person remembering how.
  • A record of every program Programs are documented with sign-ins and event records, filed in shared drives by term.
  • A decision log Leadership decisions are written down with the date and the reason, so new leaders can see why things are the way they are.
  • Structured selection Applications are reviewed and applicants are interviewed against shared criteria.
  • Shared inboxes Partners write to role inboxes, not personal accounts, so conversations survive leadership changes.
  • Role cards and named backups (being built) Leadership roles are being written up as role cards, with backups.
  • End-of-term handoff (being built) Terms close with a written handoff and an archive.
  • A Board of Directors (being built) Our bylaws provide for a volunteer Board. Seats are not yet filled.

How partnership works

  • Partnership does not include access to student names, contact details or rosters.
  • Students choose whether to stay in touch with a professional they meet.
  • Partnership does not include a hiring pipeline or guaranteed introductions to candidates.
  • Photos of younger students are never used in partner recognition or marketing.
  • Recognition is an acknowledgment of support. It does not mean BRIDGE endorses a product, and partners do not control program content.
  • We may decline support that does not fit our mission or would put students in an awkward position.
